# Shock ranges for inverse stress tests and scenario ensembles

For Mass (serving and demo) and Hridhin (dashboard). Generated by `python -m evaluation.shock_ranges`:
shipped `b3_roll4`, val 2020. Full tables: `evaluation/results/shock_ranges.{md,json}`.

## Ranges (dashboard ShockSpec format, box in degrees as `boxFromDegrees(lat0, lat1, lon0, lon1)`)
| family | box | components | physically plausible | **model-trusted (use for sliders/ensembles)** | kit shock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bay of Bengal temperature | 8, 20, 82, 94 | t2m `add` dT; tcwv `percent` 5·dT | −2 … +3 K | **−1 … +1 K** | +3 K (85.6 % stable) |
| Arabian Sea wind | 8, 20, 65, 75 | u10 and v10 `percent` p | −50 … +30 % | **−20 … +30 %** | −40 % (89.5 % stable) |

- **Trusted** means that on at least 95 % of the 354 val windows, the response stays within the kit's 5 std
  stability limit, and every output is valid.
- **Beyond the trusted range,** responses still pass validity (100 %), but 6–23 % of windows exceed 5 std, always
  in rain inside the box. Label such scenarios "outside the model's trusted range".
- **Physical basis:**
  - Bay of Bengal marine heatwaves reach about +1 to +3 K, and cool wakes after cyclones about −1 to −2 K.
  - Moisture follows at 5 %/K, which is the kit's ratio and below Clausius–Clapeyron (~7 %/K).
  - Monsoon breaks weaken the Arabian Sea low-level jet by up to about half; active spells strengthen it by
    about a third.
- **Suggested scenario ensemble (5 members per family, all trusted):**
  - Bay of Bengal temperature: −1, −0.5, +0.5, +1 K, plus the unperturbed base.
  - Arabian Sea wind: −20, −10, +10, +20, +30 %.
  - The model is deterministic, so the spread comes only from the shock magnitude.

## How big are the land impacts? (JJAS windows, days 1–7, land cells, dashboard definitions)
Small, and the spread is larger than the median. At the trusted extremes:
- **Bay of Bengal +1 K:** 7-day rain change, median [p10, p90]: coastal Odisha +0.2 [−3.2, +3.4] mm, central
  India +0.3 [−2.6, +2.0] mm. Central India t2m +0.05 K.
- **Arabian Sea wind −20 %:** west coast +0.9 [−5.5, +5.6] mm, central India +0.4 [−1.3, +2.0] mm.

In a 7-day forecast, the model's response stays mostly over the ocean near the box. Inland effects are within
a few mm.

## Inverse stress tests (thresholds fixed in the script before running)
| threshold | JJAS windows eligible | crossed, plausible + trusted magnitude | crossed by any tested shock |
|---|---|---|---|
| coastal Odisha 7-day rain ≥ 115 mm (observed JJAS 2020 p90) | 114 | 2 | 7 |
| central India 7-day rain ≥ 50 % below base | 122 | 0 | 1 |
| west coast heavy-rain share up ≥ 20 points | 119 | 0 | 4 |
| central India 7-day mean t2m ≥ 1 K above base | 122 | 0 | 0 |

**Plausible threshold crossing (recommended demo case): window ending 2020-06-30, Bay of Bengal +1.5 K.**
- Coastal Odisha 7-day rain rises from 105.6 to 115.1 mm and crosses the p90 wet-week threshold.
- The response is stable in this window, and +1.5 K is physically plausible.
- Caveat: +1.5 K is just outside the trusted range (93.8 % of windows stable vs the 95 % bar).
- Inside the trusted range, the only crossings are knife-edge: 2020-08-19 (+0.5 K, 114.6 → 116.2 mm) and
  2020-06-10 (wind −10 %, 114.1 → 115.4 mm). Both bases already sit within 1 mm of the threshold.

**No tested shock qualifies: central India heat (≥ +1 K in 7 days).**
- 0 of 122 windows cross it, even with shocks outside the plausible range.
- The closest approach is +0.71 K, at Bay of Bengal +5 K (window ending 2020-06-01).
- Physically sensible: a coastal ocean anomaly does not heat the interior by 1 K within a week.

**Physical-consistency flag.** In one window (2020-08-03), a *cool* Bay of Bengal shock (−2 K) raised Odisha
rain from 107.2 to 122.4 mm. Sign-inverted responses like this exist for single windows. Show ensemble spreads,
not single members, as evidence.

## Serving compatibility
Nothing here changes the model interface. These are inputs for `/simulate` `perturbation` (a physical delta on
the last input day), and outputs remain deterministic (null shock exactly equals no shock).
