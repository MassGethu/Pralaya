# Shock ranges for inverse stress tests and scenario ensembles

For Mass (serving and demo) and Hridhin (dashboard). Generated by `python -m evaluation.shock_ranges` for the
shipped `e3_roll8_ocean` on val 2020. Full tables: `evaluation/results/shock_ranges.{md,json}`. The previous
release's tables are in `evaluation/results/shock_ranges_b3_roll4.{md,json}`; use them only after a rollback.

## Ranges (dashboard ShockSpec format, box in degrees as `boxFromDegrees(lat0, lat1, lon0, lon1)`)
| family | box | components | physically plausible | **model-trusted (use for sliders/ensembles)** | kit shock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bay of Bengal temperature | 8, 20, 82, 94 | t2m `add` dT; tcwv `percent` 5·dT | −2 … +3 K | **−1 … +1.5 K** (b3: −1 … +1) | +3 K (88.1 % stable) |
| Arabian Sea wind | 8, 20, 65, 75 | u10 and v10 `percent` p | −50 … +30 % | **−20 … +30 %** (same as b3) | −40 % (88.4 % stable) |

- **Trusted:** in at least 95 % of the 354 val windows, the response stays within the kit's 5 std stability
  limit, and every output is valid.
- **Beyond the trusted range:**
  - Every output is still valid (100 %).
  - But 7–21 % of windows exceed 5 std, always in rain inside the box.
  - Label such scenarios "outside the model's trusted range".
- **Physical basis:**
  - Bay of Bengal marine heatwaves reach about +1 to +3 K. Cool wakes after cyclones reach about −1 to −2 K.
  - Moisture follows at 5 %/K, the kit's ratio. That is below Clausius–Clapeyron (~7 %/K).
  - Monsoon breaks weaken the Arabian Sea low-level jet by up to about half. Active spells strengthen it by
    about a third.
- **Suggested scenario ensemble** (5 members per family, all trusted):
  - Bay of Bengal temperature: −1, −0.5, +0.5, +1, +1.5 K.
  - Arabian Sea wind: −20, −10, +10, +20, +30 %.
  - The model is deterministic, so the spread comes only from the shock magnitude.

## How big are the land impacts? (JJAS windows, days 1–7, land cells, dashboard definitions)
Small, and the spread between windows is larger than the median. The table gives the 7-day rain change at the
trusted extremes, as median [p10, p90].

| shock | coastal Odisha | central India | west coast | t2m change (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bay of Bengal +1.5 K | +0.6 [−3.4, +4.7] mm | +0.4 [−2.3, +2.8] mm | — | Odisha coast +0.41 K, central India +0.08 K |
| Arabian Sea wind −20 % | — | +0.2 [−1.5, +2.0] mm | +0.5 [−5.1, +7.2] mm | — |

The response stays mostly over the ocean near the box. Inland effects within a 7-day forecast are a few mm.

## Inverse stress tests (thresholds fixed in the script before running)
| threshold | JJAS windows eligible | crossed, plausible + trusted magnitude | crossed by any tested shock |
|---|---|---|---|
| coastal Odisha 7-day rain ≥ 115 mm (observed JJAS 2020 p90) | 114 | 4 | 5 |
| central India 7-day rain ≥ 50 % below base | 122 | 0 | 1 |
| west coast heavy-rain share up ≥ 20 points | 120 | 0 | 5 |
| central India 7-day mean t2m ≥ 1 K above base | 122 | 0 | 0 |

**Plausible threshold crossing (recommended demo case): window ending 2020-08-13, Bay of Bengal +1.5 K.**
- Coastal Odisha 7-day rain rises from 106.9 to 117.8 mm and crosses the p90 wet-week threshold by 2.8 mm.
- The response is stable in this window.
- +1.5 K is both physically plausible and inside the trusted range. With b3, the same magnitude was just outside it.

**No tested shock qualifies: central India heat (≥ +1 K in 7 days).**
- 0 of 122 windows cross it, even with shocks outside the plausible range.
- The closest approach is +0.57 K, at Bay of Bengal +5 K (window ending 2020-06-01).
- This is physically sensible: a coastal ocean anomaly does not heat the interior by 1 K within a week.

**Physical-consistency caveat.** Single windows can respond with the "wrong" sign. At Bay of Bengal −2 K, the
median Odisha 7-day rain change is −1.3 mm, but the p90 is +4.0 mm, so 1 window in 10 gets wetter under cooling.
Show ensemble spreads, not single members, as evidence.

## Serving compatibility
Nothing here changes the model interface. These are inputs for `/simulate` `perturbation` (a physical delta on
the last input day). Outputs stay deterministic: a null shock exactly equals no shock, and repeated identical
requests return identical forecasts.
