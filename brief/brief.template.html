PRALAYA
A digital twin of India's daily weather: ten days ahead, plus the alternate future after a shock
Track 2, Multiverse of Monsoon
Team: Mass, Jarvis and Hridhin

What makes it different. From three days of India's weather, PRALAYA rolls the atmosphere forward ten days in about a quarter of a second on four CPU cores. Apply a shock, such as a warmer Bay of Bengal, and a second timeline shows where its effect travels. Both timelines feed a farm-stress panel. Everything on screen is computed live by our own trained model: nothing is looked up, pre-rendered or downloaded.

Model design and technical approach

  • Network. A residual CNN (model/net.py): width 96, five residual blocks, replicate padding, 0.86 M parameters. Inputs: three days of the six channels, land–sea mask, orography, latitude, longitude and the season. It predicts tomorrow as a change to today, so an untrained network equals persistence.
  • Training (train.py, latitude-weighted MSE). One-step (b1), then fine-tuning through 4-day (b3) and 8-day (e3) rollouts that feed the model its own output. e3_rainstab also weights ocean temperature 4× and penalises shock responses above 3 std. 2005–2019 train, 2020 selects, 2021 is held out; selection rules were fixed before scoring.
  • Ensemble. Prime is the mean of three seeds. Branch adds seed 0's own shock response: averaging the three responses weakened how far shocks spread and failed our gate.
  • Calibration (model/calibration.py). Weights fitted on 2020 per lead, channel and land or sea blend the mean toward a smooth seasonal curve (a mean plus three annual harmonics per cell, fitted on 2005–2019) and the last observed day. It holds no daily weather and no dates. Rain keeps weight 1: the model alone forecasts it.

How we prevent drift and blur

Skill against the better baseline, 2021

Drift. Rollout training teaches the model to correct its own errors: day-8 skill rises from −0.20 to −0.07, and the ensemble and calibration bring it to −0.01, level with the better baseline.

Day-8 spatial variance as a share of observed, 2021

Blur. The model predicts a change to today, the rollout loss punishes smooth averages, and rain is never pulled toward climatology. The ensemble had to keep all six channels in band to ship.

Failure modes

  • Day 8 only matches the baseline. Mean day-8 skill is −0.01: a typical window wins at day 4, not reliably at day 8.
  • Extreme rain is under-forecast. Day-8 rain variance is 0.62 of observed; on 2020 the served model's largest day-8 rain was 49 mm/day where 250 fell.
  • The wind shock spreads weakly. The Arabian Sea wind shock spreads outward by days 4–8 in 61% of 2021 windows (warm Bay: 97%).
  • Rare rain spikes after a shock. In 4% of warm-Bay windows a response tops 5 std, always in rain near the box.
  • Not a physics model. Physics-aware means persistence-anchored steps, terrain inputs and non-negative rain, with no conservation laws. Responses show model sensitivity, not causation.
  • Untested hardware. Timed on ARM CPUs only; x86 is unmeasured, with 8× headroom under the 5 s limit.

Interactive map and timeline

  • India on the model's own 32 × 36 grid with coastlines and cities; seven variables (rain, temperature, wind speed, both wind components, pressure, moisture).
  • Views: Prime, Branch, a signed Difference with a zero-centred scale fixed across days, and Swipe. A 10-day timeline plays the rollout.
  • Shocks: draw a box or type coordinates and add a physical change (K, mm/day, m/s, Pa, kg/m²) or a percentage. Presets reproduce the organizer's two shocks exactly. A zero check confirms an all-zero shock changes nothing.
  • The multiverse: every Branch is kept on a shelf (up to eight), recallable in one click, and All futures shows each Branch's difference map side by side.
  • Ripple reach: the share of cells outside the box that changed, per day, so a judge can watch a shock travel.

Inverse search and preparedness

The scenario service asks the reverse question: which tested shock pushes a region past a rainfall target? It returns the lowest-cost qualifying shock or the closest miss, and Replay as Branch runs it through the full model.

For coastal Odisha (17–23°N, 82–88°E, days 1–7 from 2 July 2020), Prime gives 115.1 mm. Of 13 tested shocks, only +2 K over the Bay of Bengal crosses a 117 mm target (117.5 mm); at 120 mm it is the closest miss, 2.5 mm short. With an illustrative 15 mm/day water need, supplying 0, 14 or 28 mm leaves a worst shortfall of 6.5, 2.0 or 0 mm.

Assumptions and limits

  • The indicators compare two simulated futures. They are not drought declarations, yield estimates or losses, and use no crop, soil or irrigation data.
  • A 1° daily grid cannot resolve farm-scale conditions, hourly heat or cyclone tracks, and extreme rain is under-forecast.
  • A research prototype, not a replacement for IMD forecasts or agronomic advice. Next: the same comparison along sea routes for shipping.
